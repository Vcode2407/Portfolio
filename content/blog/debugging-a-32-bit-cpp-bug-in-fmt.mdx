The fmt issue only appeared on 32-bit architecture. That immediately made it more interesting than an ordinary formatting mismatch.

## The Symptom

Hexadecimal floating formatting produced corrupted high-word behavior in the fallback `uint128` implementation.

## The Root Cause

`format_hexfloat` masked rounded bits with bitwise NOT, but the fallback `operator~` truncated to 32 bits. On i686, that meant the high word was corrupted while 64-bit validation could still look fine.

## Validation

The fix had to pass on both 32-bit and 64-bit targets. Architecture-specific bugs are dangerous because they reward testing only the machine you happen to own.

This contribution reinforced a debugging habit I trust: reduce the problem until the architecture difference has nowhere to hide.
